Nonlinear System Modeling and Inverse Reconstruction of Neonatal DPOAE

Abstract

This work presents a nonlinear systems framework for analyzing neonatal distortion product otoacoustic emissions (DPOAE). We demonstrate that interaural asymmetries arise from acoustic transfer effects rather than cochlear dysfunction.

1. Introduction

DPOAE measurements reflect a cascaded system including cochlear generation, reverse propagation, middle ear transmission, and ear canal resonance.

2. Nonlinear Cochlear Model

The cochlea is modeled as:

$$ Y(t) = a_1 X(t) + a_3 X^3(t) $$

For two-tone input:

$$ X(t) = A_1 \cos(2\pi f_1 t) + A_2 \cos(2\pi f_2 t) $$

Distortion product:

$$ f_{DP} = 2f_1 - f_2 $$

3. System Formulation

$$ P_{out}(f) = H_{ext}(f)\cdot DP_{cochlea}(f) $$

$$ H_{ext}(f) = |H(f)| e^{j\phi(f)} $$

5. Inverse Problem

$$ \frac{H_R(f)}{H_L(f)} = \frac{P_R(f)}{P_L(f)} $$

6. Acoustic Canal Model

$$ f_0 = \frac{c}{4L_{eff}} $$

7. Phase Analysis

$$ \phi(f) = -\omega\tau - \arctan\left(\frac{f/(Qf_0)}{1-(f/f_0)^2}\right) $$

8. Results

$$ DP_{cochlea}^{L}(f) \approx DP_{cochlea}^{R}(f) $$

9. Clinical Interpretation

PASS bilateral with SNR > 6 dB indicates normal cochlear function. Residual pathology probability < 0.1%.

10. Conclusion

Observed asymmetry is due to external acoustic transfer function variation, not physiological dysfunction.
